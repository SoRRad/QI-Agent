SQUIRE 2.0 (Standards for Quality Improvement Reporting Excellence) is the
reporting standard for improvement work. Writing to it from the start is
easier than retrofitting, because it asks for things — context, the rationale
linking intervention to mechanism, the study of the intervention as distinct
from the intervention itself — that are painful to reconstruct afterwards.

## Section order

**Title and Abstract**
1. **Title** — indicates this is an improvement study and names the aim.
2. **Abstract** — structured per the target venue's headings.

**Introduction** — why did you start?
3. **Problem Description** — the local problem, and its size.
4. **Available Knowledge** — what is already known, including prior attempts.
5. **Rationale** — the theory or framework linking your intervention to the
   expected mechanism of improvement. This is the section most often skipped
   and most often asked about by reviewers.
6. **Specific Aims** — the aim statement, with its five elements.

**Methods** — what did you do?
7. **Context** — the setting, and the contextual elements a reader would need
   in order to judge whether this would work where they are.
8. **Intervention(s)** — described in enough detail that someone else could
   reproduce it, plus the team.
9. **Study of the Intervention(s)** — how you assessed whether the intervention
   caused the observed change. Distinct from the intervention itself.
10. **Measures** — measures chosen, their operational definitions, and their
    validity and reliability.
11. **Analysis** — the methods used, including the statistical process control
    methods and their rules.
12. **Ethical Considerations** — including the IRB or QI determination.

**Results** — what did you find?
13. **Results** — the evolution of the intervention over the PDSA cycles,
    process and outcome measures over time, unintended consequences, and any
    missing data. Run and control charts belong here, annotated.

**Discussion** — what does it mean?
14. **Summary** — key findings and their relation to the rationale.
15. **Interpretation** — mechanism, comparison with other reports, and the
    difference between observed and expected outcomes.
16. **Limitations** — including limits to generalisability and to internal
    validity, and efforts made to minimise them.
17. **Conclusions** — usefulness, sustainability, and implications.

**Other Information**
18. **Funding** and conflicts of interest.

## Candidate venues

Maintained in `/content/venues.json` with deadlines and scope, and matched
against a project by the meeting matcher.

**Journals**
- *BMJ Open Quality* — improvement reports, explicitly SQUIRE-aligned.
- *Journal of Graduate Medical Education (JGME)* — the natural home for
  trainee-led and GME-context improvement work.
- *BMJ Quality & Safety* — higher bar; wants generalisable findings.
- *American Journal of Medical Quality*.
- *Pediatric Quality & Safety* — paediatric settings.
- *Joint Commission Journal on Quality and Patient Safety*.
- *Academic Medicine* — where the contribution is to education.

**Meetings**
- Institutional GME QI symposium — the expected first venue.
- IHI Forum.
- ACGME Annual Educational Conference.
- Specialty society annual meetings, and their regional or resident sections,
  which are usually the most realistic first abstract for a trainee.
- AAMC regional meetings.

Deadlines are the binding constraint, not scope fit. A project that would have
been accepted somewhere and missed the deadline is the most common way trainee
improvement work goes unpublished.

## What the drafter will and will not do

The SQUIRE drafter assembles a draft from the project record, the PDSA log and
the charts, in the section order above. It marks every section requiring author
input, and it does not invent results. Where a section has no source material
in the project record, it says so rather than producing plausible text.

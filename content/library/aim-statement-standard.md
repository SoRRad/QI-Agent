An aim statement is a commitment, not a description of an interest. It answers
"how good, for whom, by when" in language specific enough that a stranger could
tell, on the deadline, whether you met it.

## The five required elements

Every aim statement must contain all five. The intake validator checks for each
one individually and names the ones that are missing.

1. **Direction and magnitude, as a number** — never "improve". State whether the
   measure goes up or down, and by how much. "Reduce from 34% to 15%" is an aim;
   "improve documentation" is a wish.
2. **Baseline value, with the period over which it was measured.** A target
   without a baseline cannot be assessed, and a baseline without its period
   cannot be trusted — three weeks in July is not the same claim as a year.
3. **Population** — which patients, which unit, which service. "Our patients" is
   not a population. The population is the denominator's inclusion rule stated
   in words.
4. **Deadline, as a calendar date.** "By the end of the academic year" drifts.
   "By 30 June 2026" does not.
5. **Measure definition — explicit numerator and denominator.** If two people
   would count differently, the aim is not yet written.

## A passing example

> Reduce the proportion of discharge summaries signed more than 48 hours after
> discharge on the Hospitalist service at University Hospital from **34%**
> (baseline, 1 July – 30 September 2025) to **15%** by **30 June 2026**.
>
> **Numerator:** discharge summaries with a signature timestamp more than 48
> hours after the recorded discharge date and time.
> **Denominator:** all discharge summaries for patients discharged from the
> Hospitalist service in the measurement period.

Element by element: direction and magnitude (34% → 15%, a reduction); baseline
with period (34%, Q1 FY2026); population (patients discharged from the
Hospitalist service at University Hospital); deadline (30 June 2026); measure
definition (numerator and denominator both stated as countable rules).

## A failing example

> Improve resident handoff communication in the ICU to enhance patient safety
> this academic year.

This is the most common shape of a failed aim: it is agreeable, it is about a
real problem, and it cannot be assessed. Element by element:

1. **Direction and magnitude — missing.** "Improve" and "enhance" carry no
   number. There is nothing to succeed or fail against.
2. **Baseline — missing.** No current value and no measurement period, so the
   size of the problem is unknown and any target would be arbitrary.
3. **Population — inadequate.** "The ICU" names a unit but not which patients,
   which service, or which handoffs — shift change, transfer out, or both?
4. **Deadline — missing.** "This academic year" has no calendar date, and in
   practice means the end of the rotation of whoever is asked.
5. **Measure definition — missing.** No numerator and no denominator.
   "Communication" as written is not a countable thing; it would have to become
   something like "handoffs using the standard template" before it could be
   measured.

The repair is not rewording. Each missing element is a decision someone has to
make — usually with the clinical owner and the analyst — before the project can
start.

Most things labelled PDSA are not PDSA. They are a change, implemented once, at
full scale, followed by a hope. The discipline is what makes the cycle worth
anything.

## Prediction is the whole point

**Write down what you expect to happen, with a number, before you run the
cycle.** This is required: a cycle cannot be marked done without its
prediction, and the Study step shows your prediction and your result side by
side.

The reason is not bookkeeping. A prediction is the only thing that makes a
result informative. If you did not say in advance what you expected, then
whatever happens will feel roughly consistent with your theory, and you will
learn nothing — you will simply narrate the outcome. A wrong prediction is the
most valuable output a cycle can produce, because it means your model of the
system was wrong in a specific, locatable way.

A usable prediction is quantitative and falsifiable: "template use will rise
from about 20% to about 60% within two weeks, and the three night-float
residents will not use it at all" beats "we think this will help".

## Keep the tests small

The first cycle should be small enough to run this week, on one unit, with one
willing team, and to abandon on Friday at no cost. Scale comes after the theory
survives. Teams reliably run their first test too large, and the cost is not
wasted effort — it is that a large failed test cannot be diagnosed, because too
many things differed at once.

Ask: what is the smallest test that would still change my mind?

## Study means comparison, not reporting

The Study step is where the prediction and the result are compared, explicitly.
Two things get recorded: what happened, and what that says about the theory.
"Uptake was 30%, not the 60% predicted; the gap was entirely night shift, who
never received the briefing" is a study. "Uptake was 30%" is a measurement.

## Act: adopt, adapt, or abandon

Every cycle ends in one of three decisions, recorded:

- **Adopt** — the theory held. Make the change standard for this population and
  move to the next problem, or scale the same change to the next unit.
- **Adapt** — the theory partly held. Change the intervention and run the next
  cycle. Most cycles end here.
- **Abandon** — the theory did not hold, or the intervention cannot work in this
  system. Record why. An abandoned cycle with a clear reason is a real result
  and belongs in the writeup; the registry keeps it so the next cohort does not
  repeat it.

## Keep the log

The cycle log is the project's evidence and, later, the Methods section of its
writeup. Reconstructing it at the end from memory produces both a worse
manuscript and a worse project, because the reconstruction quietly smooths over
the cycles that failed — which are the ones that explain what actually
happened.
